# Bean & Co. | UK Market Entry Strategy

**Independent strategy consulting case study | October 2026**  
**Client:** Bean & Co. — a **hypothetical** premium UK coffee chain with 40 stores concentrated in London and South-East England.  
**Tools:** Excel, public-source desk research, market screening, scenario analysis and financial modelling.

> **Executive recommendation:** Prioritise **Bristol** for targeted site search and commercial due diligence, with an initial focus on **Clifton / Whiteladies Road** and **Gloucester Road / Stokes Croft**. Open **one pilot store only if** a fully costed, site-specific investment case passes the agreed decision gates. Do not commit to a multi-store rollout upfront.

## The business question

With limited expansion capital, **which of Manchester, Birmingham, Leeds, Bristol or Edinburgh should Bean & Co. enter next, and how should it enter?**

This project separates **city prioritisation** from **site investment approval**. A high-scoring city is a reason to investigate locations—not a reason to sign a lease.

## Headline findings

| City | Balanced screening score | Outcome |
|---|---:|---|
| **Bristol** | **69.47** | **First among fully evaluated cities** |
| Birmingham | 46.89 | Second |
| Leeds | 36.31 | Third |
| Edinburgh | 33.89 | Fourth |
| Manchester | Not ranked | Comparable property economics unresolved; remains an active challenger |

The weighted score uses **Customer attractiveness (50%)**, **Market attractiveness (25%)**, and **Economics / feasibility (25%)**. Scores are comparative screening outputs, **not** forecasts of sales, profitability or investment return.

**Robustness:** Bristol leads in **all six tested weighting scenarios** among the four fully evaluated cities. The narrowest lead is only **~2.04 points** against Birmingham when economics are weighted most heavily. This is a targeted sensitivity test, not proof that the ranking holds under every plausible assumption.

## Recommended entry strategy

1. **Prioritise Bristol site diligence** in the two shortlisted neighbourhood corridors; validate footfall, daypart demand, competition, planning/use and catchment characteristics.
2. **Build a decision-grade site P&L** with verified rent, rateable value, business rates, service charge, fit-out costs, labour and VAT treatment. Reassess demand assumptions against local evidence.
3. **Open one proof-point store only if investment hurdles are met.** Consider a **1 + 1 + 1**, KPI-gated expansion rather than precommitting to three sites.
4. **Keep Manchester under review** until comparable occupancy economics are available. Do not interpret missing information as a zero score or as evidence that Manchester is unattractive.

## How the analysis was carried out

 An issue tree divided the question into customer demand, market/competition, and economics/feasibility. The city-screening decision was explicitly separated from the site-level investment decision.

 Public demographic and economic data, broad café supply proxies, competitor mapping and sampled commercial property listings were compared at a defined local-authority/council-area screening level. Such administrative boundaries are not the same as a store's actual walking or spending catchment.

 Three weighted dimensions generated the balanced ranking. Six alternative weighting scenarios tested sensitivity. Missing Manchester economics were left unresolved, rather than estimated without support. Edinburgh's valid zero economics score was retained in sensitivity calculations.

 Gross till sales were converted to simplified net revenue, with COGS, labour, payment/packaging and other operating costs deducted. Sampled asking rent provided an initial occupancy-cost reference, but absent rates and service charges were **not** treated as zero.

 The result is a **priority city and staged diligence plan**, not a lease authorisation or a claim of forecast returns.

 The resulting recommendation balances market attractiveness with financial feasibility and the risks associated with incomplete site-level information.

## Evidence standards and known limitations

- **Fictional client:** No Bean & Co. operating history, proprietary till data or actual management approval exists.
- **Mixed provenance:** Some metrics are official observations; others are calculations, proxies, model assumptions or consulting judgements. See the source log and assumptions document.
- **Incomplete primary-source traceability:** The working source log preserves existing classifications and documented URLs, but some individual source URLs, publication dates and access dates still require completion. Do not mistake it for a fully independently verified bibliography.
- **Property snapshot:** Sampled asking rents are not agreed leases. Site availability, planning permissions, incentives, rateable values and service charges require refreshed verification.
- **Model simplifications:** VAT treatment, staffing/labour rules, customer volume and operating expenses require validation before any real-world investment decision.
- **Sensitivity scope:** Six weight combinations were tested; uncertainty in raw data, transaction volumes, prices and costs can materially change conclusions.
